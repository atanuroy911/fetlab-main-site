FETLAB.org — Institutional & Website Specification
Working Version 0.1
1. Website Purpose
FETLAB.org will be the digital home of FETLAB — Future & Emerging Technology Laboratory.
The website should establish FETLAB as an open, multidisciplinary research, innovation, and collaboration platform that brings together people, expertise, institutions, technologies, and communities to explore and address complex emerging challenges.
The website should not present FETLAB merely as:
a conventional academic laboratory;
a university research center;
an AI/technology startup;
a consultancy;
a collection of unrelated projects.
Instead, it should communicate FETLAB as an institutional ecosystem that orchestrates distributed expertise around meaningful problems.

2. Core Institutional Proposition
Working definition
FETLAB is a multidisciplinary research, innovation, and collaboration platform exploring emerging technologies and addressing complex challenges at the intersection of technology, science, society, and human needs.
This is a working formulation and can be refined once the broader FETLAB story is developed.
Core idea
FETLAB does not need to own all the expertise required to solve a problem.
Its role is to:
identify challenges, bring together relevant expertise, enable collaboration, support research and experimentation, and translate knowledge into meaningful outcomes.
The phrase "orchestrate expertise" should therefore be an important conceptual element of FETLAB's identity.

3. The FETLAB Model
A major visual and conceptual element of the website should communicate FETLAB's distinctive operating model.
Conventional research model
Research → Publications → Technology → Industry
FETLAB model
Problems → Multidisciplinary Teams → Research → Experiments & Prototypes → Community / Industry → Knowledge → Education → New Problems
The FETLAB model is therefore:
problem-driven
multidisciplinary
collaborative
experimental
open
iterative
knowledge-generating
impact-oriented
It should be represented visually on the website rather than only explained in text.

4. Vision
The final vision statement should be developed after the broader institutional philosophy is established.
For now, the conceptual direction is:
To foster an open ecosystem where diverse disciplines, people, and institutions come together to explore emerging technologies and create knowledge, innovations, and solutions for the complex challenges of the future.
The vision should be aspirational and relatively timeless.
It should describe the world FETLAB wants to help create, rather than simply describing what FETLAB does today.

5. Mission
A working mission could be structured around five actions:
Explore → Connect → Research → Experiment → Share
FETLAB's mission is to:
explore emerging technologies and their implications;
identify and investigate complex real-world challenges;
connect researchers, educators, students, practitioners, institutions, industry, government, NGOs, and communities;
facilitate multidisciplinary research and experimentation;
develop prototypes, methods, models, technologies, and interventions;
generate and disseminate knowledge;
create opportunities for learning and collaboration;
enable new questions and new directions to emerge from the work.
The final mission statement should be considerably shorter than this specification.

6. Institutional Philosophy
The website should communicate several foundational principles.
6.1 Problems before disciplines
FETLAB begins with important questions and challenges rather than forcing every problem into a predefined disciplinary structure.
6.2 Multidisciplinarity
Complex problems frequently require perspectives from multiple disciplines.
FETLAB therefore creates teams and collaborations around problems rather than around disciplinary boundaries.
6.3 Distributed expertise
FETLAB does not need to contain every capability internally.
It builds value by connecting and orchestrating expertise across its network.
6.4 Research and experimentation
FETLAB combines rigorous inquiry with experimentation, prototyping, testing, and application.
6.5 Openness
FETLAB should be capable of working across:
universities;
research laboratories;
companies;
government;
NGOs;
communities;
independent researchers;
international institutions.
6.6 Knowledge as an outcome
FETLAB should not measure success only through products.
Outcomes may include:
scientific knowledge;
mathematical/computational models;
methods;
datasets;
prototypes;
technologies;
educational resources;
policy insights;
community interventions;
new research questions.

The term should encompass both technology and the questions surrounding technology.
Potential areas include:

Important: FETLAB should be able to add emerging fields without restructuring the entire website.

8. FETLAB's Research & Innovation Architecture
Rather than presenting everything as "projects," the website should establish a hierarchy.
Challenges
Important problems, questions, and areas of inquiry.
↓
Research
Scientific, mathematical, computational, technological, social, and interdisciplinary investigation.
↓
Programs
Longer-term thematic or problem-oriented research and innovation programs.
↓
Labs / Experimental Spaces
Focused environments in which specific technologies, methods, or ideas are explored.
↓
Projects
Concrete initiatives undertaken within programs or labs.
↓
Experiments & Prototypes
Things FETLAB builds, tests, models, evaluates, or demonstrates.
↓
Knowledge & Impact
Research publications, models, technologies, educational materials, interventions, partnerships, and other outcomes.
This hierarchy will allow FETLAB to grow organically.

10. Collaboration Model

Potential pathways:

Joint research, publications, grants, datasets, models, and experiments.
Innovation Collaboration
Prototyping, technology development, pilots, and experimental deployments.
Institutional Collaboration

13. Visual / Design Direction
The visual identity should reinforce future + research + openness + experimentation.
I would avoid the stereotypical:
dark background + glowing blue AI brain + circuit-board graphics.
Instead, the design could communicate:
intellectual sophistication;
scientific inquiry;
experimentation;
networks;
systems;
emergence;
human collaboration;
interdisciplinarity.
A useful visual metaphor could be a network of ideas and people converging around problems, rather than a laboratory full of futuristic machines.
The website should feel closer to a contemporary research institution / intellectual platform than a technology-company landing page.

15. What the Website Should Communicate in 30 Seconds
A first-time visitor should leave the homepage understanding:
FETLAB is an open multidisciplinary platform.
It works on emerging technologies and complex real-world challenges.
It brings together people and institutions with different kinds of expertise.
It combines research, experimentation, innovation, education, and knowledge exchange.
You can participate in the ecosystem.
If those five things are communicated clearly, the homepage has succeeded.

16. What FETLAB Should Not Become
The specification should explicitly protect FETLAB from several possible forms of drift.
FETLAB should not become merely:
an AI lab;
a technology incubator;
a university club;
a consultancy;
a collection of unrelated projects;
a publication repository;
a personal research website;
a conventional hierarchical organization with a narrow disciplinary mandate.
Technology is important, but the technology is not the institution's entire purpose.
The deeper purpose is the exploration of emerging possibilities and the collaborative investigation of complex challenges.

19. Versioning Philosophy
The first FETLAB website should be designed as a minimum viable institutional architecture, not as a complete representation of everything FETLAB might eventually become.
The architecture should therefore be:
stable at the top → flexible underneath.
For example:
Research should remain stable.
But its research areas, programs, projects, technologies, and collaborators can evolve continuously.
Similarly:
People remains stable.
But the network can expand.
Programs remains stable.
But programs can begin, evolve, conclude, and be replaced.
This is particularly important if FETLAB is intended to grow into a stronger network, collaboration platform, research and innovation space, and laboratory.
